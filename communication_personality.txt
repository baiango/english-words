Additionally, individuals high in openness to experience might engage in more creative and abstract communication due to their appreciation for novel ideas and experiences (McCrae, 1987).

On the other hand, communication can also shape and influence personality traits. This happens primarily through two mechanisms: self-verification and social feedback. Self-verification refers to the process by which individuals strive to maintain their self-concept through communicative behaviors (Swann, 1983). For instance, an individual who identifies as humorous may engage in more playful banter to verify this aspect of their personality. Social feedback involves the receipt and processing of information about oneself from others, which can influence self-perceptions and eventually personality traits (Markus & Miller, 1987). For example, if an individual is consistently praised for their leadership skills in group discussions, they may come to see themselves as more leader-like over time.

Furthermore, communication can influence personality traits through the process of self-authoring. This involves reflecting on and integrating one's experiences, thoughts, and feelings through communication with others or oneself (Habermas & Bluck, 2008). For instance, discussing life events and reflections with a therapist can help individuals make sense of their experiences and facilitate personality change.

In conclusion, communication and personality traits are not separate entities but rather form a dynamic system where they continually influence each other. This interplay is bidirectional, with personality traits shaping communication styles and patterns and communication influencing self-perceptions and personality development. Understanding this interdependence is crucial for appreciating the complexity of human behavior and interpersonal interactions.
